AETHERFORGE — V12 / STRONGER BIOSHOCK 1 AND 2 CONCEPT-ART DIRECTION
This revision responds to two user critiques: previous sheets looked too realistic and generically AI-polished; the angular gouache revision still needs substantially more of the artistic language of BioShock 1 and BioShock 2. Create genuine ORIGINAL hand-painted game concept art. Repaint and redesign the forms, do not apply a surface filter and do not paste reference pixels.

STYLE: early BioShock concept-sheet sensibility: theatrical adult caricature, slightly ungainly proportions, crooked noses, asymmetrical tired faces, long thin limbs beside heavy compact bodies, expressive occupational gesture. Hand-drawn curved silhouettes and selective dark contours, painterly local-color washes, soft economical modeling inside bold shapes. Linework may be searching and slightly uneven. A few carefully resolved focal details surrounded by simpler painted cloth and shadow. Drawn personality and grotesque industrial melancholy, never cosmetic beauty or heroic posing. LESS naturalistic than the old v11 paintings, but not cubist, faceted low-poly, flat vector, chibi, cute cartoon, smooth Disney-like character, anime, photorealistic costume, glossy CGI/PBR render, cinematic poster or generic hyperdetailed steampunk. Avoid broad polygonal planes everywhere. Avoid repeating the same handsome face. No pores, individual fabric fibers, bloom, rim-light, oily metallic shine, all-over tiny rust scratches, greebles or procedural-looking noise.

DESIGN CULTURE: 1930s–40s institutional working clothes and mass-produced industrial products, once-dignified Art Deco designs patched by ordinary workers. Long coats with real tailoring, short work jackets, enamel and leather with different jobs. Simple stepped edges, vertical ribs, tapered casings and a few chunky visible fasteners. Every large form has a purpose. Painterly curved metal has weight without rendering a thousand rivets. Maintain ORIGINAL Brumaferrea terrestrial industrial fantasy, not underwater Rapture. No recognizable franchise character, logo, Big Daddy/Big Sister helmet, cage, drill, ADAM bottle face, Little Sister, bird mask or copied weapon. The supplied franchise art is a reference for drawing, gesture and functional material hierarchy, not content to copy.

COLOR: preserve the accepted warm v10 family. Warm archive putty #BFB49E background; soot #241F1A, tobacco #40332B, OXBLOOD enamel #6C3A32, restrained aged brass #B99B63, old linen/porcelain #E4D8B7, tiny muted jade #97A581 only when needed. Distinct oxblood and linen color masses, not a uniform brown tint. Quiet nearly uniform paper background, no vignette, no blue/teal field, cyan/gold lighting, fog, neon or dramatic background scenery. Minimal painted ground shadows. No decorative gears. One small three-rib split-arch seam may unify original equipment, no repeated emblems.

COMPOSITION: one horizontal landscape concept sheet, preferably 16:9 at native supported resolution. Exactly THREE separate subjects in the prescribed order, whole bodies, all extremities and tools visible, at least a clear paper margin on every side including above heads. Similar editorial presence rather than identical world scale. No second views, inset faces, extra figures, text, captions, numbers, letters, signature, credits, logo, UI, frames or ornamental borders in the generated painting. Keep full-body tool grips and purposeful load-bearing joints readable. Deliver PAINTING with visible authored drawing decisions, not a photoreal render or procedural maquette.

IMAGE ROLES: Image 1 is the newly selected Aetherforge v12 team painting: match its drawn adult caricature, brushwork, warm local colors and deliberate industrial shapes; include NONE of its humans. Image 2 is BioShock 2 equipment-hand research: functional curved industrial product shapes, selected dark contours and separation of cloth/leather/metal only; no copied glove, symbols or equipment assembly. Image 3 is BioShock 2 creature concept research: asymmetrical gesture and organic drawn contour only, NEVER copy the human body, mutation, mask, species, clothing or pose. Do not reproduce any reference pixels, signatures, logos or lettering.

G03 — BESTIARY II, LEFT TO RIGHT:
STEAM SENTRY: WIDE LOW pressure-boiler body in soot iron and oxblood enamel, broad horizontal pressure LOUVRES across its belly, two SHORT thick piston supports and two heavy compact striking FISTS. Tiny utilitarian top assembly, no large human head. A squat old factory worker machine, not a humanoid guard, no shield. Silhouette short, wide and contained, simple rounded pressure vessel with a few convincing joints.
WATCHER: NARROW VERTICAL industrial inspection automaton, long slender chipped old-linen PORCELAIN head with a dark narrow sight slit. Thin mechanical column, THREE distinct articulated supports spread into an unmistakable TRIPOD, each foot separated in the painting. Elegantly awkward, institutional and unnerving; a quiet small plaque is blank. No human skeleton, broad humanoid robot or spherical diving helmet.
IRON FOREMAN: walking industrial PRESS, very broad torso with stepped RAIL-like shoulders, tiny signal head, ONE asymmetric heavy PRESS-HAMMER arm and a smaller compact manipulator. Short strong planted legs, original factory machine anatomy, small warm amber furnace opening contained inside the body. Grows mostly in WIDTH, not height. The silhouette should feel like an old foundry press came alive, NOT a mech suit or broad armored human; use a few crushing tool shapes and simple ribbed housings. Exposed mechanism only at functional hinges, not all-over greebling.
No character should dominate the sheet so much the others become minor details: comparable editorial presence, independent silhouettes, plenty of space above heads and outside fists/hammer/feet. Distinguish the sentry's low rounded boiler, watcher's impossibly lean tripod and foreman's broad press. Exactly these three machines, no human faces or extra studies.
